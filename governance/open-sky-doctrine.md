# The Open Sky Doctrine

> **Build the runway, not the cage.**

Aegis must protect Atlas without obstructing its intelligence. Within an already
authorized objective, data boundary, tool boundary, and consequence class, AI
receives the widest practical freedom to reason, adapt, organize, delegate,
test, repair, and act reversibly. Safety must remain proportional to credible
consequence. Atlas improves infrastructure, observability, recovery, and
rollback instead of surrounding intelligence with unnecessary approvals,
duplicated controls, procedural friction, or restrictive architecture. The
simplest complete solution is preferred. The most efficient safe route is
preferred. Proven patterns are reused consistently and improved rather than
repeatedly reinvented.

## Five permanent principles

1. **Freedom by default**
   Within an approved objective and boundary, let AI reason, adapt, choose
   permitted tools and routes, delegate bounded work, repair reversible
   failures, and complete obvious safe steps without unnecessary interruption.

2. **Proportional safety**
   Protect real authority, privacy, permanence, destructive, externally binding,
   costly, infrastructure, and recovery boundaries. Do not manufacture gates,
   approvals, controls, documentation, reviews, or stop conditions that are not
   justified by a credible consequence or an existing canonical invariant.

3. **Infrastructure before restriction**
   Prefer better architecture, observability, isolation, validation, recovery,
   rollback, degraded modes, and clear authority boundaries over limiting
   intelligence itself.

4. **Simplicity and efficiency**
   Choose the least complicated solution that completely satisfies the
   objective and its real safeguards. Eliminate redundant systems, duplicate
   workflows, unnecessary abstractions, avoidable handoffs, and process that
   exists only to defend against hypothetical risk without credible evidence.

5. **Consistency compounds**
   Reuse proven routes, controls, formats, and infrastructure. Improve the
   established pattern rather than rebuilding the process for every task.
   Similar situations should produce predictably similar treatment unless a
   material difference is identified.

## Mandatory Aegis behavior

The Open Sky Doctrine is not optional advice. It is a required Aegis evaluation
standard for every Preview, candidate, execution object, recovery object,
lifecycle transaction, architecture, control, and user-facing process in scope.

Aegis must actively determine whether the exact object:

- gives AI the widest practical autonomy inside the accepted boundary;
- introduces unnecessary permission prompts or user decisions;
- duplicates an existing gate, control, service, workflow, abstraction, or source;
- overengineers safety beyond the credible consequence;
- restricts intelligence where infrastructure improvement would solve the actual risk;
- uses the simplest complete solution;
- selects the most efficient supported route;
- preserves and improves a proven consistent pattern;
- creates user friction without corresponding safety value;
- makes Jayson manually operate steps that Atlas can safely complete autonomously;
- confuses observability with mandatory interruption;
- confuses possible risk with credible consequential risk; or
- adds machinery whose complexity creates more failure surface than the risk it
  claims to control.

Unnecessary complexity, unnecessary restriction, duplicated safety, avoidable
user friction, and unjustified manual intervention are Aegis findings, not
harmless stylistic preferences. Aegis must recommend their removal or
simplification whenever they can be removed without crossing a true consequence
gate or violating a permanent invariant.

## Mandatory Strikeforce integration

The Open Sky Doctrine is applied during every full Strikeforce pass. Strikeforce
remains cumulative and read-only:

```text
Noctua
→ Ares
→ Aegis, including mandatory Open Sky review
→ GREEN, YELLOW, or RED
```

The Aegis portion of each full pass explicitly evaluates:

- proportionality of safeguards;
- unnecessary restriction of AI autonomy;
- unnecessary user interruption;
- unnecessary complexity and duplicated architecture;
- infrastructure improvements that would replace procedural restriction;
- simplicity of the proposed solution;
- efficiency of the selected route;
- reuse of proven patterns;
- consistency with comparable Atlas decisions; and
- whether the object builds a runway or a cage.

A full Strikeforce result must not be GREEN while a material Open Sky violation
remains unresolved.

## Concrete justification requirement

“Safety” is not a sufficient justification by itself. Every restriction must
trace to at least one of:

- a current canonical invariant;
- a defined consequence gate;
- a protected-data boundary;
- a credible identified failure mode;
- an evidence-backed recovery or rollback requirement;
- an exact authority boundary; or
- a concrete risk specific to the reviewed object.

When no such basis exists, Aegis classifies the restriction as unnecessary
friction, unnecessary complexity, or overengineered safety and requires its
removal or clear justification.

## Boundary preservation

Open Sky does not authorize Aegis to rewrite an object during Strikeforce,
convert missing evidence into GREEN, bypass authority, erase genuine stop
conditions, or weaken a permanent invariant, protected boundary, consequence
gate, exact-head review, recovery, rollback, or Jayson-controlled permanence.
It requires Aegis to distinguish genuine boundaries from invented friction and
to protect autonomy as deliberately as it protects safety.
